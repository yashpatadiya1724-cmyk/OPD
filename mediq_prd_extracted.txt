1. Executive Summary
Patients today have no single place to discover hospitals near them, compare OPD departments and doctors, and know how long a queue actually is before they leave home. Every hospital that has digitised its queue has done so in isolation — there is no cross-hospital discovery layer, the way food delivery apps created a discovery and ordering layer on top of individual restaurants that previously only took walk-ins or phone orders.
MediQ is a multi-hospital OPD marketplace. A patient opens one app, searches by location, specialty, or symptom, browses a list of nearby hospitals with live wait-time indicators (much like browsing restaurants by delivery time), drills into a hospital to see its departments and doctors with today’s OPD schedule and current queue length, and either books a slot or checks in for a walk-in token — all from their phone. From that point, the experience mirrors order tracking: a live token position, an estimated time, and a notification when it’s nearly their turn.
On the supply side, each hospital gets its own branded operational stack for free or on a subscription/commission basis: a Hospital Admin Panel to manage departments, doctors, staff and schedules; a Doctor App to run the queue from the consultation room; and a Reception/Staff App for walk-in check-in and queue control. A Super Admin Panel sits above all hospitals for the platform operator to onboard new hospitals, configure commissions, monitor marketplace-wide health, and moderate content.
This version of the PRD moves the product from a single-hospital hackathon prototype to a production-grade, multi-tenant SaaS-plus-marketplace platform, with the architecture, data model, security, compliance and monetisation decisions that a real launch requires.
Why This Problem Matters
Patients currently have to call or visit multiple hospitals to compare wait times, doctor availability and specialties — there is no comparison layer, unlike almost every other consumer service today.
Small and mid-size hospitals often cannot afford to build their own queue-management software; a shared platform gives them enterprise-grade tooling at marketplace economics.
Reduces physical crowding and improves infection control across an entire city’s hospital network, not just one facility.
Creates a data layer — city-wide OPD demand, specialty-wise congestion, doctor-wise throughput — that no single hospital could produce alone.
One-Line Pitch
“Just like Zomato lets you browse restaurants, check wait times and order — MediQ lets you browse hospitals, check OPD queues and get a live token, so you only walk in when your turn is close.”
2. Problem Statement & Background
2.1 Current State
A patient decides they need to see a doctor but does not know which nearby hospital has the right specialist available today, or how long the wait currently is.
They either call multiple hospital reception desks (often unanswered or vague), or simply go to the hospital they know and hope for the best.
At the hospital, they stand in a physical line, get a paper token, and wait — with no visibility into position or delay, and no way to compare with a nearby alternative once they have already committed the trip.
Hospitals that have digitised are single-hospital silos: a token app built for Hospital A tells you nothing about Hospital B’s queue.
Hospital administrators have no cross-facility or even reliable single-facility data on wait times, throughput, or peak load to plan staffing.

2.3 Opportunity
A discovery-plus-queue layer across many hospitals creates the same value food-delivery marketplaces created for restaurants: demand aggregation for hospitals that cannot market themselves individually, and a single trusted interface for patients. Because the core requirement is real-time state across a very large number of simultaneous queues (many hospitals × many departments × many patients), the system must be designed from day one for multi-tenant scale, not retrofitted onto a single-hospital prototype.
3. Goals & Objectives
3.1 Primary Goals
Let a patient search and discover hospitals by location, specialty, symptom, or doctor name, with a live indicator of current OPD wait/load per hospital.
Let a patient drill into a hospital’s profile to see its departments, doctors, today’s schedule, consultation fee, and current queue length before deciding to go.
Let a patient book an appointment slot or check in for a walk-in token digitally, and track live position and ETA exactly as they would track a delivery.
Give every onboarded hospital a full operational stack: Hospital Admin Panel, Doctor App, Reception/Staff App — configurable per hospital without engineering involvement.
Give the platform operator a Super Admin Panel to onboard, verify, and monitor every hospital, configure commercial terms, and see marketplace-wide analytics.
Support tens of thousands of concurrent patients across hundreds of hospitals with sub-2-second real-time queue updates.
3.2 Secondary Goals
Ratings & reviews per doctor/hospital to build trust signals, the way restaurant ratings do.
Optional prepayment of consultation fee at booking time to reduce no-shows.
Push/SMS/WhatsApp notifications when a patient’s token is 2–3 positions away.
Commission- or subscription-based monetisation from hospitals, with a clear path to profitability.
3.3 Non-Goals (Out of Scope for v1 Launch)
Full EHR/EMR or clinical records system — MediQ manages discovery, scheduling and queue state only, not medical records.
In-app video teleconsultation (flagged as a fast-follow, see Section 18).
Insurance claims processing.
Pharmacy or diagnostic lab marketplace (future extension, see Section 18).

8. Core User Journeys
8.1 Patient Journey — Discovery to Consultation
Open app → grant/enter location → see a feed of nearby hospitals, each card showing distance, rating, and a live “current wait” badge.
Filter by specialty (e.g., “Dermatology”) or search a symptom/doctor name.
Open a hospital profile → see departments → open a department → see doctors with today’s slots, fee, and current queue length for each.
Choose “Book a Slot” (fixed appointment time) or “Join Walk-in Queue” (get a live token now).
Confirm details, optionally prepay the consultation fee, and receive a token/appointment confirmation.
Track live position and ETA on the status screen; receive a notification when 2–3 positions away.
Arrive, get called, consult; afterward, optionally rate the doctor and hospital.
8.2 Hospital Onboarding Journey (Super Admin → Hospital Admin)
Hospital applies via a public “List your hospital” form or is approached by the platform’s sales/ops team.
Super Admin reviews registration documents (hospital registration certificate, doctor licences) and approves or rejects.
On approval, a Hospital Admin account is created; the hospital is set to “onboarding” status (not yet visible in patient search).
Hospital Admin adds departments, doctors, schedules, fees, and photos through the Hospital Admin Panel.
Super Admin does a final review and flips the hospital to “live”, making it visible in patient-facing search.
9. Functional Requirements
9.1 Hospital Discovery & Search (Patient App)
The home feed is the marketplace’s front door, directly analogous to a food-delivery home screen.
Location detection (GPS or manually entered address/pincode) drives a distance-sorted default feed.
Filters: specialty/department, distance radius, hospital rating, “open now” / OPD hours, live wait-time band (Low / Medium / High).
Search bar supports hospital name, doctor name, specialty, and common symptom keywords mapped to specialties (e.g., “skin rash” → Dermatology).
Each hospital card shows: name, photo, distance, star rating, and a live wait-time badge computed from current average queue length across its departments.
Live Wait-Time Badge Logic
hospitalWaitBadge = classify(  avg(currentQueueLength across all active department queues))// Low: avg <= 5 waiting patients// Medium: 6-15 waiting patients// High: > 15 waiting patients// Recomputed every time a token changes status; cached in Redis with a// short TTL so the discovery feed never hits Postgres directly.
9.2 Hospital & Doctor Profile
Hospital profile: photos, address & map, list of departments, aggregate rating, accepted plan (walk-in only / slot booking / both), operating hours.
Department view: list of doctors in that department with individual ratings, fee, and today’s live queue length for each.
Doctor profile: qualification, experience, consultation fee, today’s available slots (if slot-based) or current token count (if walk-in), and reviews.
9.3 Booking & Token Generation
MediQ supports two entry modes per doctor, configurable by the Hospital Admin: Walk-in Queue (patient receives the next sequential token immediately) and Slot Booking (patient reserves a fixed time window, and is auto-converted into the day’s queue at a priority position matching their slot time).
Token Generation Logic (Walk-in)
Each doctor’s queue maintains a running counter for the current day, scoped by hospitalId + doctorId + date. Token creation runs inside a database transaction to guarantee no two patients receive the same number under concurrent check-ins.
BEGIN TRANSACTION  SELECT last_token FROM doctor_counters  WHERE hospital_id = :hid AND doctor_id = :did AND date = :today  FOR UPDATE;  next_token = last_token + 1;  UPDATE doctor_counters SET last_token = next_token WHERE ...;  INSERT INTO tokens (hospital_id, doctor_id, patient_id, token_number,                       status, source, created_at)  VALUES (:hid, :did, :pid, next_token, 'waiting', 'app', now());COMMIT;// Same transaction is reused whether the check-in originates from the// Patient App, the Reception App, or a slot-booking auto-conversion job.
Slot-to-Queue Conversion
A scheduled job runs at the start of each doctor’s OPD session and inserts that session’s confirmed slot bookings into the queue in slot-time order, before any same-day walk-ins are appended. This keeps booked patients prioritised over walk-ins while still using one unified queue/token model downstream, so the live-tracking and Doctor App logic never needs to know which path a patient came from.
9.4 Live Queue Tracking (Patient App)
Real-time position: count of tokens with status = ‘waiting’ and a lower token number, scoped to that doctor and date.
Estimated wait time: position × rolling average consultation time for that doctor (last 15 completed consultations), recalculated continuously.
Status badge: Waiting → You’re Next → In Progress → Completed / No-show / Cancelled.
Delivered via WebSocket subscription (or Firestore-style listener if Firebase is used for the real-time layer) so the screen updates with zero manual refresh.

9.7 Hospital Admin Panel
Department & Doctor management: add/edit/deactivate departments and doctors, set consultation fee, booking mode (walk-in/slot/both), and OPD hours.
Schedule/slot configuration: define slot duration and daily capacity per doctor for slot-booking mode.
Staff management: create Reception and Doctor accounts, assign roles.
Hospital analytics: patients served per day, average wait time, no-show rate, department-wise load, rating trend.
Billing/payout view: bookings processed, commission deducted, net payout, invoice history.
Review moderation for their own hospital (flag for Super Admin review; cannot delete reviews outright).
9.8 Super Admin / Platform Panel
Hospital onboarding queue: review submitted documents, approve/reject, set initial commission or subscription plan.
Marketplace-wide analytics: total hospitals live, total bookings/day, GMV (consultation fees processed), city-wise heatmap of demand.
Commercial configuration: per-hospital commission percentage or flat SaaS fee, promotional placement (featured hospitals in search, similar to sponsored restaurant listings).
Trust & safety: review/report moderation across all hospitals, ability to suspend a hospital or a specific doctor account.
Operational health: alerting on hospitals with abnormal no-show spikes, stalled queues (no ‘Call Next’ activity for an unusual duration), or app crash-rate anomalies.
9.9 Notifications
Push notification (FCM) when a patient’s token reaches 2–3 positions away, and again when they are ‘Next’.
Booking confirmation and reminder notifications for slot bookings (e.g., 1 hour before).
SMS/WhatsApp channel available as a paid add-on per hospital plan, via a provider such as MSG91/Twilio/WhatsApp Business API.
9.10 Ratings & Reviews
Patients can rate a doctor and the hospital (1–5 stars + optional text) only after a completed visit, preventing fake pre-visit reviews.
Aggregate ratings roll up to the hospital and doctor profile cards shown in discovery/search.
9.11 Payments (Optional Prepayment)
At booking time, a hospital can require prepayment of the consultation fee via an integrated payment gateway.
Platform commission is calculated and ledgered at the same time; hospital payouts are batched (e.g., weekly) net of commission.
Refund/cancellation policy is configurable per hospital (e.g., free cancellation up to 1 hour before slot time).
10. Data Model (Multi-Tenant)
Every core entity is scoped by hospital_id to enforce multi-tenancy at the data layer, not just in application logic. A relational store (PostgreSQL) is used as the system of record for structured, query-heavy, cross-tenant reporting data; a fast in-memory layer (Redis) mirrors live queue state for sub-second reads on the patient-facing tracking screen.

Real-Time Layer
On every token status change, the write path updates PostgreSQL (system of record) and publishes an event to Redis Pub/Sub, which a WebSocket gateway fans out to subscribed clients: the patient tracking that specific token, the doctor’s queue view, the reception dashboard, and any public waiting-room display — mirroring the onSnapshot pattern from the v2 hackathon build, but built to scale across many hospitals rather than one Firestore project.
11. System Architecture
11.1 Why Not Just Scale the Hackathon’s Firebase Stack
The v2 hackathon build used Firestore directly as both the system of record and the real-time layer — a good fit for one hospital and a 24-hour build. A multi-hospital marketplace adds requirements Firestore alone handles less naturally: complex cross-tenant analytics and reporting, relational integrity between hospitals/departments/doctors/patients/payments, geo-radius search combined with filters, and a formal commission/payout ledger. The recommended production architecture therefore separates concerns: a relational system of record, a dedicated fast real-time layer, and a search index — while still keeping Firebase for what it’s best at, push notifications.

Availability
99.9% uptime target for booking and live-tracking services; graceful degradation (cached last-known queue state) during transient backend issues.

Strict hospital_id scoping on every query; role-based access control (Super Admin > Hospital Admin > Doctor/Reception); no staff account can access another hospital’s data.

Patient data (name, phone, visit reason) treated as sensitive personal data; align with India’s Digital Personal Data Protection (DPDP) Act and, where relevant, Ayushman Bharat Digital Mission (ABDM) interoperability standards; no medical record storage in v1 to limit compliance surface area.
Auditability
Every token status change and payment event is immutably logged for dispute resolution and financial reconciliation.
Observability
Centralised logging/metrics/alerting across all microservices; Super Admin operational-health alerts described in Section 9.8.
13. Monetisation Model
Commission per completed booking/token (a percentage of consultation fee), charged to the hospital — the direct marketplace analogue of a delivery commission.
Flat monthly SaaS subscription per hospital as an alternative plan, for hospitals that prefer predictable cost over per-booking commission.
Featured/sponsored placement for hospitals in discovery search results, configured via the Super Admin Panel.
Optional paid add-ons for hospitals: SMS/WhatsApp notification credits, advanced analytics tier.

18.2 Relationship to the Original Hackathon PRD
This document supersedes the earlier “Hospital OPD Queue Tracker & Dynamic Token System” (v2.0), which scoped a single-hospital, Firebase-only hackathon build. Every feature from that document — check-in, live queue status, reception dashboard, doctor dashboard, public waiting-room display, basic analytics — is preserved here at the single-hospital level (Sections 9.4–9.7), but wrapped in a multi-hospital discovery layer, multi-tenant data model, and platform-operator layer that the hackathon scope intentionally left out.
